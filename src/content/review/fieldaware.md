FieldAware targets a specific kind of contractor: the one with multiple locations, dozens of techs, and complex scheduling requirements. If that describes your business, FieldAware has capabilities that smaller tools cannot match.

The multi-location support stands out. You can manage separate companies or branches from a single account. Each location gets its own dispatch board, inventory, and reporting. Corporate gets a consolidated view.

Workflow automation lets you set up rules for common scenarios. When a job of a certain type gets created, assign it to the right crew automatically. Send notifications at specific points in the process. Escalate unresolved issues after a set time.

The API is genuinely open. If your business uses custom software, you can build integrations that connect FieldAware to your existing systems.

The tradeoff is complexity. Implementation takes weeks, not days. You will need a project manager or at least someone dedicated to the setup. The price reflects that, starting around $149 and going up from there.

FieldAware is not the right choice for a small crew. But for a contractor operating across multiple cities with fifty techs in the field, it is one of the few tools that scales to that level.
